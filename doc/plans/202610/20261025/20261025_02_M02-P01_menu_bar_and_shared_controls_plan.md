# M02-P01 — A menu bar and the small shared control set (2026-10-25)

> Status: in_progress
> Branch: `phase/M02-P01-menu-bar-and-controls` (from the M01-P07 tip)
> Approval: the owner's decision of 2026-10-25 (UI toolkit option A: keep the hand-drawn client and build a small shared control set; the menu bar comes next). G3 and G4 are pending.

## Goal

One sentence: the window gets a classic top menu bar (File, Session, View, Help) whose entries are exactly the actions that exist today, each showing its key, reachable by keyboard and mouse, drawn by one small shared control set that the Allow / Deny buttons, the session list and the hint line use too.

## Scope

1. **Menus.** File: New session (Ctrl+N), Accounts (Ctrl+A), Quit (Ctrl+Q). Session: Next / Previous session (Ctrl+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab), Start screen (Esc), one item per listed session (Alt+1 to Alt+9), Allow / Deny the pending approval (Y, N), Copy selected lines (Ctrl+Shift+C), Paste (Ctrl+V).
   View: Older output (Page Up), Newer output (Page Down), Newest (End). Help: Keys, About. Nothing is listed that does not work: an entry that cannot act now is greyed with its reason written beside it ("needs two sessions", "nothing is selected", "no approval is pending").
   The first-row text (provider, account, model, state, mode) stays as it is: a status bar was not needed.
2. **Keyboard.** F10 opens the bar, Alt+F, Alt+S, Alt+V, Alt+H open a menu, Left / Right change menu, Up / Down move over the entries that can act, Enter runs, Esc closes; Ctrl+Q quits. **Mouse:** a click on a title opens it, a click on an entry runs it, a click elsewhere closes it; every hit box is measured and kept inside the window margins.
3. **Shared controls** (`gui/controls.rs`): one drawing and hit-test module for labels, buttons and list rows (the bar, the entries, the Allow / Deny buttons, the session list, the hint line); no second drawing path for these. The prompt row stays in the transcript text. No new crate; the idle client stays under 50 MiB and the activation under 100 ms.
4. **Where it shows:** the product window always; the measurement client only with `--menu` (so the earlier pixel measurements keep their layout), and the final measurement is repeated with `--menu`.
5. Small follow-ups that fit: none planned (close and rename session need host operations that do not exist yet for rename; close is a purge and needs a confirmation design). Not started: M01-P08.

## Tests (red first, pixels and the ui dump)

Closed bar drawn and not clipped at the left; F10 and Alt+letter open menus (dump: open menu, items, keys, enabled and reasons); arrows and Enter run New session and Accounts; Esc closes; a click on a title and on an entry works and a click on a greyed entry shows its reason and does nothing;
with sessions: next / previous and a session item switch sessions, Allow / Deny are greyed without an approval and answer with one; Ctrl+Q ends the window; at scale 1 and 2 every hit box lies inside the window and no title boxes overlap; the sign-in code is still never drawn; whole suite.

## Measurement and risks

Re-measure idle RSS, hidden growth, activation and restart p95 with `--menu` at scale 1 and 2 against the same limits. Risk: moving the text area down by one bar row changes every y coordinate (click boxes, selection, approval buttons); the dump carries window coordinates so the older tests keep reading them.

## S03 — The frame cost in the noisy case (task 013, 2026-10-25)

Measured first (Xvfb, 10 accounts, one session printing 100 mixed-script lines/s, `--ui`): the noisy case costs about 13 % of a core since P07-S01 (commit eeae7e2; 1.07 % at 93bbb6a), with and without the menu bar (`--ui` 14.10 %, `--ui --menu` 13.65 %), so the bar and the label drawing are not the cause. One frame (10 fps) is 12.9 ms: layout 10.9 ms (the 10 new lines, about 1.1 ms each, are shaped once;
unchanged lines are already kept by text), paint 2.0 ms, present about 0. Callgrind: 99.8 % of `draw_ui` is cosmic-text shaping; the font search (`FontFallbackIter`, a scan over the installed faces and every monospace face for each word whose characters the default font lacks) and the per-word shape plan are the two parts.
A bench outside the product (private) gives per line: 250 to 850 us for ASCII, 500 to 1900 us with Korean / ideographs, about 110 us if one named font is forced (but then Korean and ideographs come from other fonts).

Design (same pixels, less search; amended after the first build, 2026-10-25): (1) the first monospace face is named once and every line is shaped with that name instead of the family `monospace` (checked at start: the name must give the same glyphs as `monospace` for all printable ASCII, else the old family stays). The family `monospace` made every word go through all monospace faces; with the name, an Advanced line of ASCII costs about 12 us instead of about 240 us in the bench.
(2) A character that face lacks is shaped alone once with `monospace`, the face it lands on is remembered per character (a face is addressed by family name, weight, style and stretch, so faces that share all four get distinct family names once at start), checked once with the name, and the line gets one attribute span per run of characters on another face. A character that does not check out keeps the old search.
(3) Left to the old search, by an explicit `monospace` span over the whole cluster: combining marks, joiners, variation selectors and skin tones with the character before them, right-to-left joining scripts, Indic, Thai, Jamo, flags. So the pixels are the old ones by construction, and the lookup is only used where it is a lookup per character.
The first draft also gave `Shaping::Basic` to simple lines; the bench showed the named face makes Advanced as cheap (workload 57 us a line either way), so Basic was dropped: no ligature or right-to-left risk to check.
Not changed: 10 fps pacing, the line text cache, one control path. A switch `--plain-fonts` (measurement client only) turns it off, so a test can compare pixels in one binary.

Tests (red first): the dump gets a `frame` entry (frames, microseconds per part, lines shaped, characters probed, label shapes). (a) An unchanged frame shapes no line and probes no character (mutation: bypass the line cache, the test fails). (b) Pixels: a window with Korean, ideograph, Hebrew, emoji, combining-accent, symbol and ASCII lines is the same with and without `--plain-fonts`, or the test names the differing lines.
(c) The noisy case, measured by the harness with a limit of 5 % of a core (state n). Restart p95 is measured with the same counters before any claim about its cause.

Results (2026-10-25, commits 39bd2af and 7e0bedc): the noisy case is 3.70 % of a core at scale 1 (limit 5 %, met) and 6.35 % at scale 2 (missed); it was 13.8 and 18.5 %. The blit was made row by row after the font change (scale 2: 9.7 % to 6.3 %); a frame at scale 2 is now layout 1.4 + fill 1.0 + blit 3.4 ms, and the lines move every frame, so a cached image would not help: the next lever is a design change for the lead. Pixels equal (one binary with and without `--plain-fonts`; the row blit against the old blit).
Restart: measured with the frame counters, not a regression: the end of P07 measures 114.2 ms p95 on today's machine state, the head 111.4 ms (the first frames cost 46 to 50 ms in a cold process, the rest is start and connection); the 64 and 72 ms of the P07 report were a quieter machine. Whole suite 109 passed. Details in the report.

## S04 — The window brings the host to its own build and keeps the sessions (tasks 019 and 014, 2026-10-25)

Today (`gui/mod.rs reconcile_host`): a host of another build is replaced only when it holds no session and no sign-in; with sessions the window only says "close it with `lico host stop`", and a session that went `disconnected` cannot be brought back from the window (only `lico session resume ID`). Facts from the code: the registry (`registry.rs`) keeps provider, account, provider session id, generation and mode of every provider session in one file and the next host brings them back `disconnected` with their history and sequence; `session.resume` (`core::session_resume`) takes the current generation, makes it +1 and starts the provider again
(codex `thread/resume`, claude `--resume ID`); a session without a provider session id yet answers `bad_request`; synthetic sessions are not in the registry. `host.stop` is refused with sessions unless `force`.

Options weighed (the owner decided on 2026-10-25 20:5x, so only the last is built): (A) ask in the window "restart the host now?" - an extra question nobody wants; (B) replace automatically only when every session is idle, otherwise ask - still a question; (C) a hand-over from the old host to a new one (the new adopts the old one's sessions and provider processes) - the most work and a new wire between two builds, which is the very thing that differs;
**(D, decided) the host's build hash differs -> the window replaces the host by itself, always, and resumes what was alive.** It uses what exists: registry, `host.stop`, `session.resume`; no new wire except one optional field.

Design.
1. **Where.** `lico gui` without `--socket` (the product). The first check is quick and before the window: same build -> nothing. Another build -> the window opens at once and a worker thread does the rest, so a waiting turn never keeps the window from showing; it reports to the loop with an event (header line, then "the host is a new one": the loop connects to the new socket, starts a reader for it, clears its subscription and shows the open session again).
2. **Settling.** Nothing is cut off silently: a session that works (`activity` working) or waits on an approval is waited for, polling `status` every half second, at most 60 s (the header says "Updating the host after the running turn..."); then the host is restarted anyway and the line says how many turns were interrupted. A sign-in in progress (`logins_running`) is never ended: wait for its end or 5 minutes, then the host is left as it is and the line says so ("...not restarted: a sign-in was still in progress"). The waits are shortened by an environment variable for the tests only.
3. **Stopping.** `host.stop` with `force` and a new optional `build` (stop only if the host still has this build: a compare-and-stop through the socket, so two windows cannot kill the host that the other just started; the answer is `build_changed`). An old host that rejects the field is asked again without it after a second status check; a host too old for `host.stop` (`unknown_op`) is ended once by SIGTERM on the pid its own `status` named (never by process name),
then the socket is waited for to vanish (3 s); if it does not, the line says which process is left.
4. **Starting and resuming.** `ensure_host()` starts the new host (the same call as at the first start). The sessions that were alive in the old host (provider sessions in state `running`) are resumed one by one with the generation the new host reports (+1), in the worker thread. A session that was `disconnected` or `exited` before stays so. The header line: "Restarted the host (new build); resumed N sessions." and, for the ones that did not come back, "...; M could not be resumed (reason)". Never hidden.
5. **Two windows.** The second window sees either the old host (its `host.stop` carries the build it saw: the first one's stop wins, the second gets `build_changed` or no host, looks again and finds the new build, and does nothing more) or the new one. The resuming is done only by the window that stopped. A test starts two windows against one old host.
6. **Resume from the window (task 019 item 1).** Session menu: "Resume session" (Ctrl+R), greyed with a reason ("no session is open", "the session is running", "the session has ended", "the host does not know a conversation to resume yet" for the `bad_request` case); uses `session.resume` with the row's generation; the list shows the new state at the next tick. The same key works from the keyboard, the entry from the bar.
7. **014 inside it.** The "run `lico host stop`" line is shown only when the replacement could not be done, with the reason (a sign-in in progress, the old process did not end). A `bad_request` to `session.create` becomes "The host does not understand this request: it is an older build." (a defensive message); an old host without a build id counts as old (already so: the ids differ).

Tests (red first). A fake codex (the simulator of the codex adapter tests behind a `codex` script on the PATH of host and window) gives real provider sessions on an older-build host (a copy with one byte more). (a) Two sessions, idle: after `lico gui` starts the host is the new build, both sessions are running under generation +1, the header reads "Restarted the host (new build); resumed 2 sessions." and an input to each is answered (history and sequence continue). (b) A session before that was disconnected stays disconnected. (c) A running turn is waited for (with the wait shortened), and one that does not end is cut after the limit and counted in the line.
(d) A sign-in in progress keeps the host and says so. (e) An old host without `host.stop` is ended by pid and replaced. (f) Two windows at once: one host, one set of resumes, no double kill. (g) The menu entry and Ctrl+R resume a disconnected session (greyed otherwise, with the reason). (h) `session.create` answered `bad_request` shows the sentence. The S06 stale-host tests stay green (their expectations change to the new line where they now describe the old behaviour).
Measured, not read from the docs: what resume restores with the real `codex` and `claude` (context kept? a turn that was running? an approval that was waiting?) - one small experiment per CLI on this machine, stated in the report.

### S04 — what was built and what was measured (2026-10-25)

Added after the first plan commit, by the owner's decisions and the lead's review (task 020).
1. **The rule is "the build differs", not "the build is older".** A build hash has no order, so a window of an older build (for example the previous installed one) replaces a newer host as well; the latest is always the window's own build.
2. **A window already open when another window replaces the host** starts, stops and replaces nothing after its own start. When its socket ends it says "The host was replaced; reconnecting...", tries the same socket path without starting anything and attaches again from the latest screen when a host answers there; otherwise the line says to close the window and run `lico gui` again. Test: window A open on host H1, window B replaces H1 by H2; afterwards A is attached to H2, H2 was started once, H1 stopped once, A started and stopped nothing.
3. **No provider process of the old host survives.** `host.stop` ends every session's provider group before the host exits (the parent-death signal that `spawn_provider_process` sets reaches only the direct child, not what a provider started). The window also collects the old host's children by pid before the stop and ends what is left by that pid (never by name); the header names a process that could not be ended.
4. **What a resume restores (real CLIs on this machine, small throwaway sessions).** codex `thread/resume`: the conversation is kept (a word told in turn 1 is answered in turn 3); a turn that was cut is marked `interrupted` and its request is known to the model; an approval that was waiting is not asked again and its command did not run; the provider ends by itself when its input closes. claude `--resume`: the same session id and the conversation are kept; the permission request that was waiting is not asked again and the command did not run; a claude that loses its input **keeps working** (a 25 s tool finished and the turn went on to a result 28 s later, writing its file) - this is why the host stops the provider group instead of closing its input, or a resumed session would meet a second claude on the same conversation. The header therefore says how many turns were interrupted and how many waiting approvals were not kept.
5. **Mutations** (tests have teeth): disabling the follow-the-new-host path, dropping the leftover ending, and dropping the provider-group stop in `host.stop` each turn a test red (the last needed a provider that starts a process of its own; with only a provider the parent-death signal hides the difference).

### S04 — review round 022 (2026-10-25)

1. **Helpers of the old host's providers (64b8235).** The providers of the real old build die with their host (parent-death signal on the direct child); the helpers they started stay in the dead leader's process group. The window now also ends every process whose group was led by a child of the old host (start time checked against pid reuse).
2. **`host.stop` always ends the host (05ff6e0, 44ce4bb).** The provider pids are kept outside the session locks; the stop reads sessions with `try_lock` (a poisoned lock is read through), a watchdog ends the process at the latest 2.7 s after the reply, and the registry save runs under a budget of 1.5 s before the reply, not under the watchdog. Tested with a held, a poisoned and a free session lock, and with a child process that ends without a reply.
3. **The two-window test that failed under load (0aa16dd).** Cause found with the env-gated decision trace (`LICO_UPDATE_TRACE`): the window that stopped the old host waited for the socket to be empty (3 s); the other window, having lost the race, started the new host in the gap, so the first window never saw an empty socket, called the old host "not ended", resumed nothing and left the sessions disconnected. The old host is now gone when nothing listens or when another process answers on the socket. A fake host that hands the socket over to a successor in one step makes this deterministic.
4. **Header wording.** A host that ends without any answer to `host.stop` is reported as "The host ended and a new one answers (new build)." (what was seen), not as replaced by another window.

## S03 — font review round (task 021, 2026-10-25)

1. **The font shortcut is default-deny (bfb4108).** A line takes the per-character face lookup only if every character is on a whitelist of neighbour-independent characters (`INDEPENDENT` in `gui/fonts.rs`, 22 ranges: Latin and its extensions, Greek, Cyrillic, general punctuation, symbols and arrows, CJK ideographs and kana, precomposed Hangul, fullwidth forms, emoji pictographs). Anything else (right-to-left scripts, combining marks, joiners and zero-width characters, variation selectors, regional indicators, skin tones, tags, Indic and Thai scripts, jamo, private use, unassigned planes) sends the whole line to the old search, shaped as before; so does a line with a character whose face did not check out. The first version listed the classes it knew to be hard and let the rest through; the new one lets through only what it knows to be safe, so a class nobody thought of is shaped the old way.
2. **Tests.** A whitelisted corpus and a seeded random differential (1200 lines of two and three whitelisted characters) are compared with the old search by glyph tuples and the exact list of draw rectangles (equal rectangles are equal pixels; the two font systems name duplicate faces differently, so font ids are not compared) and must not reach the old path; a corpus of 26 neighbour-dependent lines must take it whole and look the same; a structural test lists the classes that must never be on the whitelist. Mutations (a: the Hebrew block put on the whitelist; c: a character with an unknown face shaped per character instead of sending its line whole; d: whitelisted lines longer than 12 characters sent to the old path) are caught by the behavioural tests; b, adding U+200B to the whitelist, is the exception: on this machine only the structural test catches it, since no line of the corpus shows a difference. The corpus depends on the installed fonts (CJK extension B has no face here, so it is not in the corpus).
3. **Two text workloads, and a changed way of measuring the limit.** The standard `--text` is mixed-script on purpose (right-to-left words and combining accents among the words: about four lines in five take the exact path, about 0.7 to 0.9 ms each). It stays as it was. A new `--realistic-text` is the same generator with an arrow and a check mark where those words were. **The 5 % noisy-CPU limit (PERF-05) is now judged on the realistic text.** The mixed-script text is measured once at scale 1 and 2 and **reported without a limit** (metric `noisy_mixed_text_cpu_percent`, classification "reported"); the change is written into the scope string of every measurement (`output`), so a reader of a dump cannot take one for the other. The hex workload keeps its limit.
4. **Numbers** (a2, `--ui --menu`, 10 accounts, 60 s window, a busy machine at load 5 to 7): realistic 3.38 % at scale 1 (met) and 5.98 % at scale 2 (missed, limit 5 %); mixed-script 10.13 % at scale 1 and 13.28 % at scale 2 (reported). S03 before the round measured 3.70 and 6.35 % with the old shortcut on `--text`. The scale-2 miss is the reason for the scroll step.
5. **Possible later optimisation, not built:** a bidi-exact hybrid, in which Hebrew and Arabic words are shaped uniformly with the face of their first character; it would need `unicode-bidi` and `unicode-linebreak` as direct dependencies. Only if a measured need appears.
6. The text libraries build with `opt-level = 2` in the dev profile (`Cargo.toml`), so the font differential takes about 3 s instead of 35 s.

## S03 — the scroll step: do not repaint what only moved (task 021 last section, 2026-10-25)

Why. Realistic text at scale 2 is 5.98 % of a core (limit 5 %). A frame there is layout 1.4 + fill 1.0 + blit 3.4 ms at 10 frames a second (the flood gate), and in tail-follow about three quarters of the lines on the screen are the same lines as in the last frame, a few rows higher. Today every glyph of every line is blended again.

Options.
- **A. The previous frame is the softbuffer buffer itself** (recommended). `Buffer::age()` is 1 when the buffer holds the last presented frame (softbuffer 0.4.8 on X11, which is the owner's session: `XDG_SESSION_TYPE=x11`; the age is 0 after a resize or a first frame). Move the retained rows inside it (`copy_within`), clear and draw only the rows that changed. No extra memory. Any age other than 1 is a full repaint with the cause "buffer age" (a Wayland compositor with two buffers will often say 2; if the measurement shows that on a machine that matters, option B is the answer for it).
- **B. A frame buffer of our own** that is kept, drawn into, and copied to softbuffer each frame. Independent of the age, but a copy of the whole frame per frame (about 11 MB at scale 2, around 1 ms, a third of what is saved) and 11 MB more resident (scale-2 idle 34.44 MiB would be about 46 MiB against the limit of 50, and a bigger window grows it).
- **C. A bitmap cache per line**, composed by copying. No age and no shift detection, but glyphs of neighbouring lines overlap by a few rows and a cached, already blended line cannot reproduce the blend order of the full repaint, so pixel equality is not guaranteed. Rejected.

Design of A.
1. **One decision function** `decide(prev, now, age, allowed) -> Cause` (enum). `Cause::Shift` is the only cheap one; the others are full repaints and are counted by name in the dump's `frame` entry (`causes`): `first`, `buffer_age`, `size`, `scale`, `fonts`, `menu`, `selection`, `panel`, `approval`, `preedit`, `markers`, `scrollback`, `line_changed`, `nothing_in_common`, `row_height`, `overhang`, `disabled` (`--no-scroll-step`, measurement client only). It is default-deny: a frame is a shift only if every item that can change pixels is in a state that is known to be harmless; anything that the function does not know about is a full repaint. A later overlay (a search highlight, a popup) must be added to the function or it stays a full repaint.
2. **What is compared.** The previous frame leaves a record: size, scale, the visible transcript as (line number, text, first row, rows, vertical and left extent of its glyph bitmaps), the same for the header line and the prompt, and whether the frame was presented. The new frame is a shift when the first lines of the new window equal the last lines of the old one (same number, same text) and everything after them is new; k is then the rows of the dropped old lines (the header may change rows too; the move is signed and may be zero). A changed line anywhere in the common part is `line_changed`. The header and the prompt are always redrawn (they sit in the redrawn rows).
3. **What is moved and what is redrawn.** The retained rows are the pixel rows that only retained lines touched in the old frame and that only retained lines touch in the new one; this uses the real extents of the glyph bitmaps (an emoji or a stacked accent reaches into the next row), not an assumption of one row per line. They are moved with one `copy_within`; every other row of the text area is cleared to the background and the runs that touch it are blended again, clipped to those rows, in the same order as the full path. The list column, the Allow and Deny buttons, the hint line and the menu bar are drawn by the same code as now, after their own regions are cleared. A fractional row height whose sums are not exact in f32 (k rows would not land on whole pixels), a glyph reaching left into the list column, and an approval bar are full repaints (`row_height`, `overhang`, `approval`).
4. **Split for tests.** `draw_ui` becomes: get the buffer, `compose(&mut buf, width, height, age)`, present. `compose` needs no window, so a Rust test can run the scripted sequence against two buffers (step on, step off) and compare every pixel after every step, with no display; one end-to-end test on Xvfb shows that the real softbuffer reports age 1 and the shift is taken, and that the final screenshot equals the `--no-scroll-step` client's.
5. **Tests (red first).** Pixel equality after EVERY step of one scripted sequence: tail-follow bursts of 1, 3 and many lines, wide and wrapped lines, lines with an emoji and stacked accents (extents beyond the row), a menu opening in the middle of a burst and closing, a resize, a selection while output flows, a scroll-back and return, a scale change, a header that changes rows, at scales 1, 2 and 1.5. The cause counters must name the path of every step (a test asserts the shift was taken where it can be and not where it cannot). Mutations that must turn a test red: one row too many; no redraw of the rows under a closing menu; no selection overlay; the new lines at the old offset after a resize; the extents ignored (a stacked-accent line).
6. **Not in this step:** markdown, the activity indicator. There is no search in the window today, so the oracle has no search step; the function is built so that one would have to be named in it.

Measure like S03 (scale 1 and 2, `--ui --menu`, 10 accounts, the realistic workload; idle CPU, activation p95, idle RSS, hidden growth), with and without `--no-scroll-step` on the same binary, and report either way; no tuning of the workload. The RSS number is stated (option A adds none).

### Results of the scroll step (task 023, 2026-10-25, commit ba29b72)

**Built as option A.** `gui/scroll.rs` holds the one decision function `decide(prev, now) -> Result<Shift, Cause>` (17 causes: `shift` and 16 reasons for a full repaint; counted by name in the dump's `frame` entry, with `kept_rows` and `scroll_step`); `draw_ui` is now the shell around `compose(&mut buf, width, height, age)`. The previous frame is the softbuffer buffer, used only at age 1. On the real X server (Xvfb, softbuffer 0.4.8) the age is 1: in a 106-frame run the dump shows 86 shifts, 17 menu frames, 2 panel frames and 1 first frame, no `buffer_age`. Retained rows come from the extents of the real glyph bitmaps, not from the line boxes; a kept line whose glyphs reach into a cleared row is blended again, clipped to those rows, in line order. Differences from the plan above, stated: (1) there is no `markers` cause; the measurement client's two marker squares are the rows above `corner` (16) and no kept row starts there. (2) `scale` and `fonts` are decided before `size` (a scale change also changes the list width, and the cause should name the scale). (3) The oracle runs at scales 1, 2 and 1.25, not 1.5: at 1.5 a row is 27 whole pixels and would behave like 1 and 2; 1.25 (22.5 pixels) is the fractional case. (4) `--no-scroll-step` is a flag of the measurement client (and of `lico_measure run`); a `repaint` command of that client drops the last frame so that the next one is a full repaint.

**Tests.** `gui::scroll::tests` (5): the decision on hand-made frames (shift by two rows, new lines only, every non-shift cause, dropped-line overhang, old prompt and new header limits). `gui::scroll_oracle` (3, scales 1, 2, 1.25): two windows get the same events, one with the step (its buffer starts as garbage and keeps its last frame, age 1) and one with `--no-scroll-step`; after EVERY one of its 88 steps the pixels of the two are compared, and the cause of every step is asserted. The script: bursts of 1 to 12 lines and 30 single lines, of twelve kinds (plain, code, Korean, emoji, stacked accents, marks above and below, wrapped, Hebrew, brackets, box drawing); a menu opening in the middle of a burst, output behind it, moving and closing; a selection made, output while selected, cleared; scroll-back and return; a pre-edit; typing; a draft with marks stacked above it; a header with marks below it, wrapped and plain; list rows added and changed; an old line edited; 200 lines at once; a resize with age 0 and one with a wrong age 1; a new buffer (age 0) and the buffer of two frames ago (age 2); a scale and font change. Where the cheap path is possible it must be taken (66 of the 88 steps are shifts at scales 1 and 2, 29 at 1.25 where a shift by an odd number of 22.5-pixel rows is declined as `row_height` or `line_changed`). One test on Xvfb (`test_frame_cost.py`): the real client follows a flowing stream, a menu is opened with the mouse and closed with Escape in the middle, the host is stopped (SIGSTOP), and the screen the steps left equals the screen of a full repaint of the same lines (`repaint`). A second test shows that `--no-scroll-step` draws every frame as a full repaint (`disabled` = frames).

**Mutations** (script `private/t023/mutate_scroll.py`, each restored after its run; all red): m0 `decide` always disabled; m1a the memmove one pixel row too far; m1b the shift one text row too many; m2 a closing menu not repainted (`prev.menu` ignored); m3 the selection overlay forgotten; m4a the size guard dropped; m4b the age guard dropped; m5a extents not shifted with the lines; m5b kept lines that overhang cleared rows not blended again; m5c extents of the dropped lines ignored; m5d extent of the old prompt ignored; m5e extent of the new header ignored; m6 the bottom rows not cleared; m7 the list column not cleared. Which test catches which: m1, m2, m3, m5a, m5b, m6 and m7 by the pixel oracle; m4a by the oracle's wrong-age step and the cause test; m4b by the cause assertion at the resize and by the age steps; m5c and m5d by the oracle and a unit test; m5e by the oracle at scale 1 only and a unit test (at 1.25 the step with the header that has marks below it is declined as `row_height`; at scale 2 it is taken and the oracle sees no difference with the extent ignored, and I did not find out why; the unit test covers it). Disclosed: the tests were written after the code, not before; their redness is shown by the stub (m0) and the mutations, and the first full run of the oracle needed three corrections of my own expectations (the window too small for a burst of twelve at scale 2, the order of the scale and size causes, the fractional-scale causes) and none of the code under test. The two guards of a resized buffer (`age` and `size`) overlap; each has its own step in the script.

**What a frame costs** (same binary, 30 s of the noisy case, realistic text, 10 accounts, from the client's own dump; every frame in both runs was a shift or a `disabled` repaint): scale 1: paint 1.48 → 0.76 ms, frame 2.92 → 2.17 ms (layout 1.3 ms unchanged); scale 2: paint 4.89 → 2.43 ms, frame 6.33 → 3.84 ms. Per shift the step keeps 519 (scale 1) and 1042 (scale 2) pixel rows of the text area. The layout (1.3 ms, the 10 new lines per frame being shaped) is now more than a third of the frame at scale 2.

The step turns the scale-2 miss into a pass, 4.43 % against a limit of 5 %: 0.57 points of headroom on one run on a busy machine, so the margin is not large. The "off" figure of scale 2 (6.73 %) is above the 5.98 % of the font round, which was taken at a lower load; the comparison inside this table is the fair one. **RSS: the step adds nothing** (option A uses the buffer softbuffer already holds; idle RSS 34.64 against 34.62 MiB at scale 2, limit 50); the hidden growth with the step is 0.2 to 0.6 MiB higher than without, inside the spread of the three launches (samples 0.54, 0.07, 0.18 at scale 1). Activation is 1 ms lower with the step, which is noise. A machine whose buffers have age 2 (a Wayland compositor with two buffers) repaints in full with the cause `buffer_age` and has the cost of before; option B is the answer for it, if a measurement on such a machine ever shows a need.
